// Scientific investigation // High order thinking // Experiment //
**	To study the effects of different liquids on beet root discs, a student prepared test tubes containing a different liquid (30% ethanol, 30% sucrose solution or distilled water) and put some beet root discs into each tube. She observed the following changes.
	30% ethanol	30% sucrose solution	Distilled water	
Observed changes	The beet root discs sank. The solution turned red.	The beet root discs floated initially and then sank. The solution remained clear.	The beet root discs sank. The solution remained clear.	

Which of the following can be deduced from the results?
(1)	Ethanol destroyed the cell membrane of the beet root discs.
(2)	The water potential inside the beet root discs was higher than that of 30% sucrose solution.
(3)	The density of the beet root discs was lower than that of distilled water.
A	(1) only				B	(1) and (2) only
C	(1) and (3) only		D	(2) and (3) only
B
Note:
The 30% ethanol turned red, indicating the red pigment of beet root cells got out of the cells in 30% ethanol. This was because the cell membrane of the cells was destroyed by ethanol.
When the beet root discs were put into the 30% sucrose solution, the discs floated and then sank. This suggested that the density of the discs was increasing. This was caused by a net movement of water from the cells (higher water potential) to the sucrose solution (lower water potential) by osmosis.
The density of beet root discs should be higher than the density of distilled water because the beet root discs sank in the distilled water.
