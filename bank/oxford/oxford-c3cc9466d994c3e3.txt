// Communication // Basic concepts //
*	Explain how tissue fluid is formed and how it may be returned to the blood circulation.												(10 marks)

-- answer --
Any seven from: 											1m x 7
At the arteriole end of the capillary bed, the pressure of blood in the capillaries is higher than the pressure of the fluid surrounding the body cells.
This forces some components of the plasma out of the capillary walls to form tissue fluid.	
Plasma proteins, red blood cells and blood platelets are too large to pass through the capillary walls. They remain in the blood. 
White blood cells can squeeze through the capillary walls and enter the tissue fluid. 
The composition of tissue fluid is similar to blood, except for the absence of red blood cells, blood platelets and plasma proteins.
As plasma proteins remain in the blood, the water potential of the blood at the venule end of the capillaries is lower than that of the tissue fluid.
Water in the tissue fluid is drawn into the capillaries by osmosis.
Excess tissue fluid drains into the lymph capillaries.
The lymphatic system helps carry excess tissue fluid to the blood circulation.
