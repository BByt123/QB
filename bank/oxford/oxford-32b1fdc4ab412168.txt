[10239659]
// Graph interpretation // High order thinking //
**	A scientist investigated the relationship between two species of Paramecium 
(P. aurelia and P. caudatum). She grew them together in a test tube. The graph below shows the changes in the populations of the two species over a period of time.
	

What is the relationship between these two species?
A	mutualism
B	predation
C	parasitism
D	competition
D	

Note:
When the population of P. caudatum decreased, the population of P. aurelia increased. This indicates that P. aurelia does not gain benefit from P. caudatum. Therefore, the relationship between these two species is not mutualism.
When the population of P. caudatum dropped to zero, the population of P. aurelia remained constant. This indicates that P. aurelia does not depend on P. caudatum for food. Therefore, the relationship between these two species is not predation nor parasitism.
While the population of P. caudatum decreased and dropped to zero, the population of P. aurelia increased and then remained constant. This indicates that P. aurelia is a stronger competitor over P. caudatum when nutrients become limited.

Nitrifying bacteria 												1m
convert ammonium compounds into nitrites and then into nitrates, which can be absorbed by plants.												1m
Nitrogen fixing bacteria in the soil or in root nodules of leguminous plants		1m
convert nitrogen gas into ammonium compounds. 						1m
Denitrifying bacteria 												1m
convert nitrates in the soil into nitrogen gas, which is then released into the atmosphere. 															1m
