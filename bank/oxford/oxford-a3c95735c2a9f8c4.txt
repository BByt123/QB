// Data handling // Experiment // Calculation //
**	A student used the following set-up to investigate the effect of humidity on the rate of transpiration of a leafy shoot.
	

He put the set-up under two environments that differed only in humidity for the same period of time. He then measured the distances moved by the air bubble and the changes in the weight of the set-up. The table below shows the results obtained.
Environment	Distance moved by the 
air bubble (cm)	Change in the weight of 
the set-up (g)	
I	1	0.3	
II	1.2	0.8	

The cross-sectional area of the graduated tube is 0.5 cm2. Which of the following can be deduced from the results? (1 cm3 of water weighs 1 g.)
(1)	The leafy shoot retained 0.2 g of water when it was put under environment I.
(2)	The humidity of environment I was higher than that of environment II.
(3)	The leafy shoot put under environment II was likely to become flaccid after the experiment.
A	(1) and (2) only
B	(1) and (3) only
C	(2) and (3) only
D	(1), (2) and (3)
D


Note:
The amount of water retained by the leafy shoot
= water absorbed - water lost
= (1 × 0.5) - 0.3 = 0.2 g
Compared to environment II, the leafy shoot lost less water under environment I. Since the rate of transpiration decreases with an increase in humidity, the humidity of environment I should be higher.
The amount of water retained by the leafy shoot under environment II
= (1.2 × 0.5) - 0.8
= -0.2 g
There was a net loss of water. Therefore, the leafy shoot was likely to become flaccid.
