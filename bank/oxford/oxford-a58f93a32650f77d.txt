**	An investigation about the sensitivity of the eye was carried out. A normal person was exposed to bright light in a room for a period of time. The lights were then turned off and a purple light spot was turned on. The person was asked to adjust the intensity of the light spot to the lowest level that he could still observe. The intensity of the light spot was recorded. The graph below shows the results. It was reported that the purple colour of the light spot was not noticeable after 10 minutes.
	

Which of the following can be deduced from the above information?
The sensitivity of the eye increased gradually over time after lights were turned off.
The sensitivity of cone cells to light was the same as or higher than that of rod cells during the first 10 minutes.
Rod cells were not stimulated during the first 10 minutes.
A	(3) only
B	(1) and (2) only
C	(1) and (3) only
D	(1), (2) and (3)
B
Note:
During the first 10 minutes, the person could see the purple colour of the light spot. This indicates that cone cells were involved in detecting the light of lowest intensity. After 10 minutes, the person could not see the purple colour. This indicates that cone cells were not involved in detecting the light of lowest intensity.

-- answer --
The eye is the sense organ for detecting light. The ear is the sense organ for detecting sound. 														1m

*	Describe what eye accommodation is. Illustrate this with daily-life examples.															(12 marks)

-- answer --
Eye accommodation is the ability of the eye to focus on objects at different distances.																	1m
It is brought about by the action of the ciliary muscles, which contract or relax to change the tension in the suspensory ligaments,								1m
thereby changing the curvature of lens for focusing light rays onto the retina.		1m
For example, when we are reading a book (or other suitable examples), we are focusing on a near object.												1m
Ciliary muscles contract. This reduces the tension in the suspensory ligaments and makes them slacken.												1m
Since the lens is elastic, it becomes more convex (thicker) and refracts light more.																	1m
When we are looking at the buildings across the harbour (or other suitable examples), we are focusing on distant objects.									1m
Ciliary muscles relax. This increases the tension in the suspensory ligaments and they become tightened. 												1m
As a result, the lens is pulled. The lens becomes less convex (thinner) and refracts light less.															1m

*	How can we detect sound? Write an essay on the processes involved in the detection of sound waves in the ear and the generation of nerve impulses.																(10 marks)

-- answer --
The pinna collects and directs sound waves along the auditory canal to the eardrum.																	1m
Sound waves cause the eardrum to vibrate.								1m
The ear bones amplify and transmit vibrations from the eardrum to the oval window.																	1m
The oval window vibrates, making the perilymph of the cochlea vibrate.		1m
Vibrations in the perilymph are transmitted to the endolymph of the central canal.	1m
Hairs of sensory hair cells in the central canal are bent and the cells send out nerve impulses.														1m
Nerve impulses travel along the auditory nerve to the auditory centre of the brain to produce the sensation of hearing.									1m
