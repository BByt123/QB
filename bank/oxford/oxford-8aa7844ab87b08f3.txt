Cell wall:
It is a thick, rigid layer covering the cell membrane. /
It is mainly made up of cellulose. /
It is fully permeable. / It allows water and all dissolved substances to pass through.
Cell membrane:
It is a thin and flexible membrane enclosing the cell. /
It is differentially permeable. / It only allows certain substances to pass through.
Cytoplasm:
It is a jelly-like fluid consisting of mainly water and proteins.
Nucleus:
It is a spherical structure bounded by the nuclear membrane. /
It contains DNA / genetic material.
Mitochondrion:
It is a structure bounded by a double membrane. / Its inner membrane is highly folded.
Endoplasmic reticulum (ER):
It is a network of interconnected membrane-bounded sacs. /
It is continuous with the outer nuclear membrane and extends throughout the cytoplasm. /
There are two types of ER, rough ER and smooth ER.	
Vacuole:
It is usually large and located in the centre of the cell. /
It is filled with cell sap. / It contains water and dissolved substances. 
Chloroplast:
It is bounded by a double membrane. /
It contains chlorophyll.

// Experiment // Communication // Basic concepts //
	Describe the procedures of using a light microscope to observe a prepared slide under high-power magnification.								(12 marks)

-- answer --
Insert a low-power eyepiece into the body tube and select a low-power objective.																		1m
Turn on the light source. Look through the eyepiece and adjust the diaphragm until the light is sufficient.												1m
Clip the prepared slide onto the stage.								1m
Watch the stage from the side and raise the stage by turning the coarse adjustment knob until the objective is at a position closest to the slide. 					1m
Look through the eyepiece and lower the stage by turning the coarse adjustment knob until the image is roughly in focus.									1m
Turn the fine adjustment knob to get a sharp focus.						1m
Move the part of the specimen you want to observe in detail to the centre of the field of view.															1m
Rotate the nosepiece to select a high-power objective. Turn the fine adjustment knob to get a sharp focus.												1m
Adjust the diaphragm to brighten the view if necessary.					1m

// Comparison // Communication // Basic concepts //
*	Compare and contrast the structure of prokaryotic cells and eukaryotic cells.															(11 marks)

-- answer --
Similarities:
Both of them are bounded by a cell membrane.						1m
Their genetic material is DNA.									1m
Differences: (any 6)											1m x 6
Eukaryotic cells are usually smaller than prokaryotic cells. /
Eukaryotic cells have a true nucleus while prokaryotic cells do not. /
DNA of eukaryotic cells is enclosed within the nucleus, while that of prokaryotic cells is lying free in cytoplasm. /
Eukaryotic cells have membrane-bounded organelles by a double membrane while prokaryotic cells do not. /
In eukaryotic cells, some ribosomes are attached to ER and some are lying free in the cytoplasm, while in prokaryotic cells, ribosomes are lying free in the cytoplasm. /
In eukaryotic cells, only plant cells have a cell wall and animal cells do not, while most prokaryotic cells have a cell wall. /
The cell wall of plant cells contains cellulose but that of prokaryotic cells does not.
