// Communication // Basic concepts //
	Give an account on the ways of transmission of infectious diseases from one person to another. Name one disease transmitted by each way.		(11 marks)
-- answer --
Ways of transmission with examples: (any 4)						2m x 4
By droplets or air:
When patients talk, cough or sneeze, droplets containing pathogens are expelled from the breathing system. Transmission of diseases occurs when these droplets deposit on the mucous membranes of the eyes, nose or mouth of another person. Transmission of diseases may also occur when suspended particles carrying pathogens or fungal spores are inhaled.
e.g. influenza / SARS / measles / tuberculosis
By water or food:
Water or food contaminated with pathogens is ingested.
e.g. cholera / gastroenteritis
By body fluids:
Pathogens in the blood of patients enter the body of another person through wounds, sharing of injection needles, transfusion or during childbirth. Pathogens in semen and vaginal secretion of the patients enter the body of another person during sexual intercourse.
e.g. hepatitis B / AIDS
By vectors:
Pathogens are picked up by some animals and transmitted to new hosts.
e.g. typhus / cholera / malaria / dengue fever
By direct contact:
Pathogens transfer directly when patients are in contact physically with another person.
e.g. athlete's foot / sexually transmitted diseases such as genital herpes

// Comparison // Communication // Basic concepts //
*	Describe the differences between viruses and bacteria as causes of infectious diseases in humans.											(11 marks)

-- answer --
Viruses can cause diseases only inside cells while bacteria can cause diseases inside or outside cells.													2m
All viruses entering the human body can cause diseases while many bacteria in the human body do not cause diseases.									2m
Viruses cause diseases by disrupting the cellular mechanism or damaging the cell membranes of the host cells, while bacteria cause diseases by producing toxins or enzymes.														2m
Bacterial infections can be treated by antibiotics and sulpha drugs while viral infections cannot.														2m
