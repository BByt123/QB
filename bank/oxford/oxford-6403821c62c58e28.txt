The disease is caused by a mutation of a gene called the CFTR gene. The CFTR gene codes for a channel protein that controls the movement of chloride ions across the cell membrane of mucus-secreting cells. The diagram below shows the movements of chloride ions and water in a normal bronchiole.

	

a	Based on the information provided, suggest why the movements of chloride ions and water are reduced in patients with cystic fibrosis.		(3 marks)
b	Patients with cystic fibrosis are more likely to have bacterial infections of the lungs. Suggest why.									(2 marks)
c	Gene therapy is a possible way to treat cystic fibrosis. It can be done by delivering a normal gene into mucus-secreting cells in lungs using a virus as a vector.
i	Suggest how the normal gene can be delivered into mucus-secreting cells in lungs using a virus.							(2 marks)
ii	Explain why the gene therapy described above does not offer a permanent cure for cystic fibrosis.						(2 marks)
iii	Explain why patients treated by the gene therapy described above would not be able to pass the normal gene on to their children.
													(2 marks)



-- answer --
a	As a mutation occurs in the CFTR gene, the shape of the channel protein produced is changed.											1m
The channel protein may become non-functional. Chloride ions cannot be transported from the mucus-secreting cells to the outside of the cells.		1m
As a result, more chloride ions remain in the cells. The water potential of the cells becomes lower. Less water moves out of the cells by osmosis.			1m
b	As the mucus of the patients is thick and sticky, it is difficult to be removed by the beating action of cilia.											1m
The mucus remains in the bronchioles for a longer time. Thus, the bacteria trapped by the mucus can grow in the lungs.						1m

ii	The normal gene will be lost when the mucus-secreting cells with the gene inserted die.											1m
New mucus-secreting cells for replacement do not have the normal gene.
Thus, the gene therapy does not offer a permanent cure for cystic fibrosis.
														1m
iii	The normal gene is delivered into somatic cells only.				1m
Their gametes still have the defective gene.						1m
