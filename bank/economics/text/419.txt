Question code: B4C19Q314
* Explain why output levels in a perfectly competitive market and a monopoly under simple monopoly pricing are different even though profit-maximising conditions in both markets are the same. Assume that the demand and cost curves are the same in both markets.	(6 marks)

Answers:
Sellers in both markets will produce at the output level where MR = MC. 	(1 mark)
However, when a monopolist practises simple monopoly pricing, MR < P. As a result, at the profit maximising output level (where MR = MC), MC < P, and this means MC < MB (because consumers will buy the quantity where MB = P).	(2 marks)
By comparison, in a perfectly competitive market, sellers are price takers and the equilibrium output level is where MC = MB.	(2 marks)
Given that the demand and cost curves are the same in both markets, the output levels in the two markets are different.	(1 mark)
(Graphical illustration is also accepted.)

(b)	Suggest TWO conditions for successful implementation of this type of price discrimination.	(4 marks)

Answers:
(a)	This can be an example of price discrimination because tuition fees for bright students are effectively lower than those for normal students even though they receive the same tuition services.	(2 marks)
It belongs to third-degree price discrimination.	(1 mark)
(b)	Conditions for successful implementation of third-degree price discrimination:
•	The tutorial schools must have some monopoly power to control prices. This means that if a tutorial school raises tuition fees for normal students (while lowering tuition fees for bright students), not all of them would be able to switch to other tutorial schools.
•	The tutorial schools are able to segment the market. In other words, it is difficult for bright students to resell their tutorial services to normal students.
•	Bright students and normal students must have different demand elasticities. Otherwise, tutorial schools cannot increase their profit by charging bright students lower tuition fees while charging normal students higher tuition fees.
•	The information cost for the tutorial schools is low, i.e., they can easily distinguish between bright students and normal students. (In fact, the information cost is low because students who want to get scholarships or tuition fee refunds will voluntarily provide their public exam results to the tutorial schools.)
(Any TWO of the above, 2 marks ( 2 = 4 marks)
