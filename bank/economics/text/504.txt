Question code: B5C04Q305
Suggest TWO reasons why the aggregate demand curve is downward sloping.
	(6 marks)

Answers:
The aggregate demand curve is downward sloping because the following effects occur simultaneously as the price level changes.
(	The wealth effect - When the price level rises (falls), the purchasing power of people's monetary assets decreases (increases). Other things remaining constant, the real wealth of households and firms drops (rises). Hence, real private consumption and investment expenditure drop (rise) and so does the aggregate output demanded.
(	The interest rate effect - When the price level rises (falls), as more (less) money is needed to finance transactions, saving decreases (increases) while borrowing increases (decreases). This raises (lowers) the real interest rate. Thus, real private consumption and investment expenditure decrease (increase) and so does the aggregate output demanded.
(	The net exports effect - When the domestic price level rises (falls), prices of domestic products rise (fall) relative to the prices of their foreign substitutes. The volume of our exports falls (rises) while the volume of our imports rises (falls). As a result, the aggregate output demanded falls (rises).
