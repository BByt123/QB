Question code: B5C06Q004
* Study the following table about the income tax system in Lucky Town.

Taxable income ($)	Total tax payment ($)	
Below 1,000	800	
1,000 - 1,999	500	
2,000 - 2,999	100	
Above 3,000	0 - 50
(Determined by a lucky draw 
at the time of payment)	
All people should pay their taxes in person at a specified time.	

Based on the table above, we can conclude that the income tax violates
(1)	the equity principle.
(2)	the certainty principle.
(3)	the convenience principle.
(4)	the economy principle.
A.	(1) and (2) only
B.	(3) and (4) only
C.	(1), (2) and (3) only
D.	All of the above

Answer: D
Explanation
Under the income tax system, the higher the taxable income, the lower the total tax payment. Thus, the income tax system violates the equity principle.
The income tax system violates the certainty principle as the total tax payment (for the taxpayers who have taxable incomes above $3,000) is not certain.
The income tax system violates the convenience principle as the timing and the method of payment are not convenient for taxpayers.
The income tax system violates the economy principle as the government has to incur the unnecessary cost of holding lucky draws. The cost may be even higher than the tax payment (which can be zero).

Question code: B5C06Q033
* Let C, Y and c% be the consumption expenditure on Good X, total income and percentage of consumption expenditure on Good X in total income, respectively. The percentage tax rate on Good X is 5%.
Which of the following statements is INCORRECT?
(1)	If all people have the same c%, the tax is a proportional tax.
(2)	If c% increases, C must increase.
(3)	If c% of the higher-income group is smaller than that of the lower-income group, Good X must be an inferior good.
A.	(1) and (2) only
B.	(1) and (3) only
C.	(2) and (3) only
D.	All of the above

Answer: C
Explanation
Average tax rate =  =  = c% × 5%
Option (1) is a correct statement. If all people have the same c%, they will have the same average tax rate. Thus, the tax is a proportional tax.
Option (2) is an incorrect statement. c% will also increase if Y decreases or the percentage decrease in Y is greater than the percentage decrease in C.
Option (3) is an incorrect statement. The demand for inferior goods will decrease as income increases. Suppose both income groups pay the same price for Good X. Even if c% of the higher-income group is smaller than that of the lower-income group, C (i.e., consumption expenditure on Good X) of the higher-income group may still be greater than that of the lower-income group. This implies that the higher-income group's demand for Good X is greater than the lower-income group's demand for Good X. In this case, Good X cannot be an inferior good.

Question code: B5C06Q045
* Which pair of economic goals CANNOT be achieved at the same time by adjusting direct taxes?
A.	Stimulating long run economic growth and stabilising the price level
B.	Stabilising the price level and solving the problem of overheating
C.	Stimulating long run economic growth and reducing income inequality
D.	Stabilising the price level and solving the problem of unemployment

Answer: C
Explanation
Option A is not the answer. Suppose there is deflation. The government can stabilise the price level by cutting the salaries tax. At the same time, it can stimulate long run economic growth by reducing the progressivity of the salaries tax. Then, both goals will be achieved.
Option B is not the answer. When there is a problem of overheating, an inflationary gap exists. The government can relieve inflationary pressure and solve the problem of overheating at the same time by raising the salaries tax.
Option C is the answer. To reduce income inequality by adjusting direct taxes, the government can raise the progressivity of direct taxes. However, this will reduce input suppliers' incentive to supply factors of production and cannot stimulate long run economic growth.
Option D is not the answer. When there is unemployment, a deflationary gap exists. The government can stabilise the price level and solve the problem of unemployment at the same time by cutting the salaries tax.
