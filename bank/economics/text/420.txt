Answers:

Question code: B4C20Q321
* Some restaurants only offer set meals on special days, such as Christmas and Easter holidays. Customers are not allowed to order à la carte (i.e., ordering dishes individually). 
(a)	Is this arrangement an example of tie-in sales? Why?	(2 marks)
(b)	Must this arrangement harm consumers? Give TWO reasons.	(4 marks)


Answers: 
(a)	This arrangement is an example of tie-in sales. This is because consumers who want to have one part of a set meal (say, the main course) are required to order all the other dishes in the same set (say, soup, salad and dessert).	(2 marks)
(b)	This arrangement does not necessarily harm consumers. Reasons:
•	Restaurants generally do not have sufficient market power and consumers have many choices. Consumers can choose to dine at restaurants that do not have such an arrangement. As a result, restaurants cannot effectively raise prices by this arrangement.
•	Because of the benefits of economies of scale, it is generally less costly to provide only set meals on peak days (such as Christmas and Easter holidays) than providing many different kinds of dishes. If the restaurants pass some of the cost savings on to consumers, consumers can benefit from lower prices.
•	Demand for a restaurant's services increases on peak days (such as Christmas and Easter holidays). Even if the prices of set meals increase on peak days, we cannot tell whether the price increase is due to an increase in demand or the arrangement. Hence, we cannot conclude that the arrangement must harm consumers.
(Any TWO of the above, each 2 marks. Accept other reasonable answers.)

(iv)	A French grape grower grows a new type of grape of exceptionally high quality. The grower will sell his grapes to only one buyer who accepts his harsh policies. A nearby winery agrees and signs a contract with him.
 	(2 marks)

Answers:
(a)	(i)	It involves resale (or retail) price maintenance. Minimum prices for the wines are imposed by the winery.	(2 marks)
(ii)	It involves exclusive territory. The wine importer is given the sole right to sell the Australian brand of wine in Hong Kong.	(2 marks)
(iii)	It involves a joint boycott. The retailers enter into an agreement to request that suppliers not deal with other retailers. 	(2 marks)
(iv)	It involves exclusive dealing. The grape grower agrees to sell the grapes to one winery only.	(2 marks)
(b)	For case (a)(i)

	OR
	The practice may benefit consumers because it saves on the cost of searching for price information about the wines. If the cost is greater than the extra money consumers need to pay due to resale price maintenance, consumers gain from the practice. Hence, it is hard to say resale price maintenance harms consumers.
	(3 marks)
	For case (a)(iv)
	Exclusive dealing may harm consumers because competition among wineries can be avoided. The winery may charge a higher price and consumers may need to pay a higher price for the wines.	(3 marks)
